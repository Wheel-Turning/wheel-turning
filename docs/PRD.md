# Wheel Turning PRD

Formerly Dharma Gates. Oct 3, 2026 · @Alex

## Problem

Western Zen dharma talks number in the tens of thousands, and almost nothing in them can be found, searched, questioned or reused. The foundational texts they lean on are scattered in the same way.

- Volume outruns attention. Centers give talks weekly, and one person can follow only a few sanghas.
- No central index exists. Talks sit in video and audio archives across YouTube channels, center websites and podcast feeds, so their content is unstructured and unavailable to any application layer. Texts sit in PDFs, scanned books and web pages, many of them needing OCR.
- Only talks that become text, in a book for example, get cited in other talks. Unless something carries a teaching further, it reaches only the few people who happened to hear it.
- A talk found online often leans on texts the listener doesn't have, or on context from a practice period the listener never saw.
- No natural-language search shows how different teachers approach the same difficulty.
- The graph of talks citing texts and texts informing talks is invisible from the level of the talk.
- Lay students get dokusan in short weekly windows, while their questions arise in thousands of ordinary moments when no teacher or tool is at hand.

## Vision and principles

Wheel Turning lets a practitioner describe what is happening in their practice and see how teachers have actually addressed it, with every passage traceable to its talk or text, its teacher or author, its sangha and a way to give. The purpose is to carry teachings further. As Red Pine puts it in his commentary on the Platform Sutra, a teaching exists to be transmitted from one mind to another, as one candle lights another. The product is free and noncommercial, any gift goes directly to the sanghas whose teaching was received, and the founder intends to form a nonprofit.

1. Passages, not paraphrase. The unit of result is a short verbatim excerpt with a link to its exact source moment or page, and, unless its teacher limits it, a link into the full transcript of the talk at that passage. A summary may say what the talks say and how they agree and differ, but every sentence in it cites the excerpts that support it, each citation shows a verbatim line the code takes from the cited excerpt, and nothing is stated that no teacher said (owner's decision of October 12, 2026, replacing the one-line synthesis).
2. Teachers are never spoken for. Different approaches stay visibly different. Blending voices into one answer would erase what makes the corpus worth searching, so the summary reports what is said in a given talk ("in a 2011 talk, Weitsman says ..."), never what a teacher believes, and keeps agreement and difference in separate sections.
3. Dana follows citation. Each passage carries its sangha's own donation link, and the sanghas cited in an answer share equally in any gift made from it.
4. Relevance alone ranks. No payment, partnership, claimed profile, upload volume or rights basis (consent, license, permission or mandate) changes where a passage appears.
5. Text only. The product stores transcripts, texts and metadata, never audio or video, and plays from the source. Student audio is never stored. A student's question is kept only as a lightly edited text, with names and identifying details removed, attributed to "Student", shown as context for the teacher's answer and searchable (owner's decision of October 10, 2026).
6. Use, ask, record, leave. Sources are used by default on the project owner's decision. The project then asks each rights holder for explicit permission, records every answer, and removes anything on request, permanently. A teacher can also choose a narrower level than full removal: excerpts only, nothing public, or a copy of their transcripts for their own use (see Display levels).
7. Honest labeling. The page never says or implies a permission or license that was not given. Credit, a source link and a way to report an excerpt appear with every passage.
8. Free and noncommercial. No ads, no paid tiers, no sale of the corpus or its data. The site's own optional dana is separate and never a condition of use.

Accessibility governs the interface. The site conforms to WCAG 3.0, which takes precedence over any other design rule in this document. WCAG 3.0 is still a Working Draft, so conformance is tracked requirement by requirement, stated honestly on a public Accessibility page, and where the draft and WCAG 2.2 differ the stricter outcome applies.

## Users

Lay practitioners are the primary user, because they are the largest audience and the ones with questions between dokusan windows. Teachers and sanghas set two constraints that later versions must meet. Rights holders are not users, but their ability to leave shapes the whole product.

| User | Role in the product | What they need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lay practitioner | Primary user for v1 | Answers to situational questions in their own words, from teachers who would stand behind the passage |
| Teacher | Quality bar from v2, and free to leave at any time | Faithful excerpts, generous context, control over their own talks, a one-message way out, and enough usefulness to prepare talks and trace references |
| Sangha or center | Sustainability from v2b | Exposure to their teachers' work, correct donation links, and a reason to supply their archive |
| Publisher or translator | Rights holder for texts | Honest credit, a link back to their source, and a one-message way to withdraw or grant permission |
| Doan-ryo and liturgy officers | Backlog | Chant references and cantillation guidance that mostly do not exist today |

In v1 the practitioners are SSZ members and a few invited teachers, not the public.

## Roadmap

Each phase tests one hypothesis and ends at a gate, so the project can fail fast. v1 ends at Gate A, the two v2 tracks run in parallel, and both must finish before Gate B.

| Phase | Hypothesis | Gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| v1: private demo | Passages teachers would stand behind, and dana links get followed | Gate A: faithful retrieval, no invented claim in synthesis, testers following dana links |
| v2a: teacher track | Teachers judge the excerpts and control their own talks | Gate B |
| v2b: sangha track | Sanghas supply archives and keep donation links right | Gate B |
| v3: gifts and texts | Single-gift payment split and licensed translations | None yet |

Gate B: roughly 15 to 25 Soto centers, a few thousand segmented talks, three or more teachers in most answers. v2a and v2b run as separate tracks, so either can slip without stalling the other.

Translation license conversations start during v1, because publisher timelines run in months and their answers decide what v3 can contain.

## v1 scope

v1 is a private demonstration for SSZ members and a few invited teachers, built to fail fast on retrieval quality, faithfulness, rights handling and the dana hypothesis.

Three kinds of material feed it:

- Talks. SSZ talks, about 262 distinct talks and 163 hours on Vimeo after removing duplicates and non-talks (inventory of October 5, 2026, which includes 5 talks from early 2023 that the SSZ site embeds but the account's public list hides), plus 14 recordings to review, all published on Vimeo, with only some on Apple Podcasts and Spotify. Recorded talks of other Soto sanghas join from sources the owner adds, whether under a license or on the owner's mandate. Teachers who have not replied are included unless they opt out, subject to Kanshin's view.
- Texts. Foundational works such as the Platform Sutra and the Dhammapada, loaded from URLs or files, run through OCR where needed, and searchable in their own section. BDK America and Terebess are the first mandated sources, alongside open-license texts.
- Reference assets. Glossaries, alias lists, lineage charts and locator lists. The pipeline uses them and the page never shows them.

**In scope**

- Transcripts of talks, segmented into talk, chant, announcement, sitting and Q&A, with only talk and teacher answers indexed as excerpts. A student's question is kept only as a lightly edited text with identifying details removed.
- Retrieval over passage-level segments of talks and, separately, units of texts.
- The answer page described below, with teacher, sangha, source link, donation link and a report link on every talk passage, and a separate Texts section.
- A full-transcript page for each talk at display level c, reached from each of its passages, showing the teacher's words with marked gaps, the attribution, source and giving links at the top, and a "Listen at" link for each passage.
- Offline transcript review files for priests or other reviewers, imported as reviewed transcript versions, and owner-run exports of a teacher's reviewed transcripts for their own use, such as books.
- A per-answer dana checklist: the donor enters a total, sees the equal division across cited sanghas, and each line opens that sangha's own donation page. No money passes through the product.
- Source setup from a URL or a file, with OCR for scans and a proofread gate before an OCR text can serve.
- Hand-listed citation resolution for a small set of known texts, with everything else shown as unresolved.
- The rights ledger: source mandates, permission requests drafted and recorded, grants, takedowns and permanent exclusions, with a public Rights and removal page and a teacher opt-out command.
- Query logging with a stated policy, read in aggregate unless a tester flags a query.

**Out of scope**

- Accounts, payments, claim-your-profile and a polished interface. Takedown is a command run on request, plus the public removal form.
- Automatic sending of permission requests and notices from a domain email address, which is in the backlog.
- Audio or video storage.
- Student audio, student names, and a student's unedited words.
- Talks from other lineages. Texts from any tradition may be loaded.

## Answer experience

A question returns passages, the closest matches first and each under its teacher's name (owner's decision of October 15, 2026), under a short cited summary in the manner of a Consensus research answer, with footnotes that resolve to talks and to texts, and a separate section of excerpts from the texts.

1. The student or teacher asks a question in situational language, such as "I keep judging myself during zazen."
2. The page shows the summary, then the verbatim passages below it, each with teacher, sangha, links to the source, a link to that sangha's dana page and a "Report this excerpt" link. When the source publishes its own transcript, the passage links to that transcript and the project shows no transcript of its own; when the project made the transcript, the passage links to the recording at that moment and to the project's transcript (owner's decision of October 12, 2026). A collapsible References list in MLA style closes the page.
3. The summary (owner's decision of October 12, 2026, moving Spec 00's post-beta item 4 before beta). It opens with a one-sentence headline answer, then a short framing paragraph, then sections for where the talks agree, where they differ, and points to keep in mind (few talks on the topic, one sangha dominating the excerpts, context a quote leaves out), and closes with a one-sentence sum-up. Each sentence cites the excerpts that support it and passes the verifier; a sentence of agreement or difference cites excerpts from at least two teachers. Every citation marker points to a verbatim quotation that the code, never the model, takes from the cited excerpt, so a reader can check each claim at once. Instead of an evidence-strength meter, the summary states how many talks and teachers it draws on. The verbatim excerpts stay below it, and when no headline survives the checks the page shows the excerpts without a summary.
4. A "From the texts" section follows the talk passages. Each excerpt shows the work, locator, author, translator, edition, page when known, a source link and a credit line. Texts never count as teachers, never enter the dana split and are not passed to synthesis.
5. Citations inside passages become footnotes. When a cited text is loaded, the footnote opens that passage in the text. Two lists close the page: the talks cited and the external texts referenced, with unresolved citations shown as unresolved.
6. A dana panel offers a gift to the site and an equal split across every sangha cited in the answer.

The credit line follows the rights basis and never claims more than it has. Where the owner relies on a mandate it names the publisher or teacher and the source. Where permission or a license is recorded it says so.

**Teacher rules**

- Default answers draw on at least three distinct teachers when each clears a relevance threshold. The system never pads to reach three. Fewer teachers means the answer says how many it found.
- In v1 the three-teacher rule is relaxed and displayed, because about three in four SSZ talks come from one teacher. The share of answers with one or two teachers is itself a measurement.
- Retrieval diversifies across speakers, so the most prolific teacher does not fill the answer.
- When a query names a teacher, as in "what has Norman Fischer said about grief," retrieval filters to that teacher and the three-teacher rule is suspended. If the corpus holds little or nothing from them, the system says so and does not substitute someone else. No summary is written in this mode, and the dana split collapses to that teacher's sangha.
- When a query names the author of a loaded text, such as Dogen, the Texts section filters to that author's works and talks retrieve normally with the name removed, so teachers' commentary on that author still appears.

**Sensitive questions.** A question that shows crisis or a health emergency gets a pointer to help before anything else. The most serious cases get a notice instead of results, with no summary line and no giving panel, and their text is never stored. Milder distress and medical questions run normally with a short notice above the answer, and distress answers have no summary line and no giving panel. The site says plainly that it shows what teachers have said and is not counseling or medical advice.

## Data pipeline and model

The pipeline turns a source into rights-cleared, segmented, glossary-corrected text with metadata. Audio and video are discarded after transcription, and a student's words are stored only after a model has lightly edited them and removed identifying details.

**Talks, in order**

1. Ingest. List talks from Vimeo or YouTube metadata, podcast feeds or SSZ storage, deduplicate on title, date and duration, and drop non-talks such as the 11-second quote clip.
2. Clear. A talk proceeds only with a recorded rights basis (speaker consent, a verified license or the owner's mandate), a known rights holder, and no opt-out or exclusion. The check repeats at every stage.
3. Transcribe. Fetch media into scratch space, transcribe once with timestamps and delete the audio. Manual captions serve only as a reference for measuring accuracy.
4. Correct. Run a glossary pass for Dogen, shikantaza, Genjokoan, Japanese and Sanskrit terms and teacher names. The glossary is a standing asset and also feeds citation resolution.
5. Segment. Label each stretch as talk, chant, announcement, sitting or Q&A. Index talk and teacher answers as excerpts. Store each student question only as a lightly edited text with identifying details removed, embedded so a search can reach the teacher's answer through it. Drop student audio.
6. Extract. Capture teacher, sangha, date, talk type, series and part, practice-period context, and every cited text or case.
7. Resolve. Match citations to canonical locators, such as fascicle and section, at three confidence levels: resolved, probable, unresolved.
8. Index. Embed passages for retrieval, with the source link and start time stored beside each.

**Texts and reference assets**

- A text is loaded from a URL or a file as a specific edition, because rights attach to the translation, not the work.
- Scans go through OCR. Pages the OCR reads poorly can be re-read by a vision model under a strict transcription prompt, and a person proofreads a sample before an OCR edition can serve. Large scans run as resumable chunked jobs.
- Texts are cut into units by verse, number, paragraph or heading, each with a canonical locator, and are searched separately from talks.
- Reference assets become glossary terms, citation locators, person aliases and lineage edges. They are never embedded or displayed.
- Fetching is polite. It obeys robots.txt, identifies itself, rate limits per host and refuses audio and video.

**Registries and fields**

- Teacher registry with aliases. Steve Given appears as Jundo (his novice priest name), Shindo and Daiho Shindo variants, and Chris Middleman is also Daiho, so a bare Daiho names no one. Kanshin appears alone and as Kanshin Allison Tait. Nomon is Tim Burnett and Kanho is Chris Burkhart, two people at Red Cedar. The seed is `config/registry.toml`. Named-teacher detection depends on this registry. A separate person registry holds the authors and translators of texts.
- Sangha registry with lineage tag, affiliation, verified flag and a donation destination per sangha. A teacher may add a second destination. The default is the destination the source video points to.
- Talk record fields: talk type, series, part, source URL, duration, rights basis, rights holder (unknown blocks indexing), source, and last-verified date.
- Source policy and rights ledger. Each source records its basis, whether it is enabled and how noticed documents are handled, with an append-only history of requests, grants, suspensions and takedowns.
- Series and part metadata let the interface say what a talk presupposes. The Genjokoan, Uji, Bendowa, Blue Cliff Record and Song of the Grass Hut series are labeled in the titles, which gives free ground truth for scoring the resolver. The list is missing Genjokoan paragraphs 5 and 6, which is worth checking.

## Rights, notice and removal

Sources are used by default on the project owner's decision, each rights holder is asked for explicit permission, every answer is recorded, and anything is removed on request, permanently. This replaces the earlier rule that no talk is indexed without its speaker's consent. The decision is the founder's, Kanshin's view is being sought, and one setting turns mandated talks off without deleting anything, so consent-first is one command away. This section is a product position, and a lawyer should review it before any wider launch.

**Three bases for use**

- Permission. A teacher's consent, a publisher's written yes, or, for talks, the sangha's written yes. Talks are given to the sangha, and sharing them is the sangha's decision rather than the individual teacher's, so a sangha's yes for its talks is permission (owner's decision of October 14, 2026). The page says the material is used with permission.
- License. The source states a license on the project's allow-list: public domain, CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA. A person reads and approves each one. Noncommercial and no-derivatives licenses wait on counsel.
- Mandate. The owner relies on a source with no license or permission, as a recorded decision. The page names the source and never claims permission.

**The lifecycle**

1. Use by default. A source is added with a recorded rationale and an attribution line.
2. Notify and ask. Each source and each teacher gets a drafted notice that says what the tool does, that the material is used on the project's own decision, how to leave with one message, and an invitation to give explicit permission. Notices are sent by hand until domain email is enabled.
3. Record the answer. A grant switches the basis to permission. A refusal or takedown removes the material and excludes it permanently. Silence is logged as silence and is never treated as permission.
4. Leave at any time. Any teacher, sangha or publisher can opt out through the public Rights and removal page, the "Report this excerpt" link on every passage, or a direct message. The named material is suspended within one business day without argument, before verifying who is asking, and restored only if the requester does not hold the rights. A removal deletes the text, embeddings and stored copies, and adds permanent exclusions covering the source, the work, the person and the content hashes, so the same material cannot be loaded again, even from another URL. Only the rights holder's own written grant lifts an exclusion.
5. Keep a ledger. An append-only record covers every mandate, notice, follow-up, grant, decline, suspension, takedown and exclusion. A public page lists each source and its status in plain words.

**Other rules**

- Student questions (owner's decision of October 10, 2026, replacing the paraphrase rule of October 7, 2026). A teacher's answer is often meaningless without its question, so a student's question is kept as context: a language model lightly edits the student's words, keeping their wording but removing names, places, contact details and other identifying details, and filler. Code checks the edit before it is stored. It is attributed only to "Student", never a name, and labeled "Question lightly edited by machine; names and identifying details removed." It is shown before the teacher's answer on the transcript and answer pages, does not count toward an excerpt's length limit, and is embedded so a search can match it, which returns the teacher's answer, never the question alone. Student audio and a student's unedited words are never stored. Talks processed before this decision need their recordings fetched again to recover their questions.
- A talk needs a resolved speaker, a registered sangha and a known rights holder. "Unknown" is a valid rights-holder value and blocks indexing until someone fills it in.
- Answers show short passages. When a talk's source publishes its own transcript, the project links to it and shows no full transcript of its own: such talks are at most at level b (owner's decision of October 12, 2026). Otherwise a talk's full transcript is shown on its own page by default, with the same attribution, source link, giving link and report link as a passage, unless its teacher has chosen a narrower display level. Full texts are not displayed: texts stay excerpt-only under Spec 08's limits. Owner's decision of October 7, 2026, after Genzen asked to be able to open the full transcript from a passage so that priests can review transcription errors and his talks might later become books.
- A document that carries another party's copyright notice is flagged on arrival, and a source can be set to hold or block such documents. Under the owner's mandate the default is to flag.
- Each tester chooses whether their questions are kept. Kept questions are disclosed, read only as totals by topic, and opened individually only when the tester flags one. A tester can switch keeping off or delete all their questions at any time, and a question not kept is deleted after 24 hours.
- The mandate is meant for a private demonstration. Turning on public exposure with mandated sources enabled raises a warning at startup and in the nightly audit, and counsel reviews the model before any wider launch.

**Display levels**

Each teacher has a display level, and a talk can be set narrower than its teacher. The default for talks on every basis is full, except that a talk whose transcript comes from its source is at most b, since the source already shows it (owner's decision of October 12, 2026). Leaving one level for another takes one message, like any removal.

| Level | What the public sees | What the project keeps | Copy for the teacher |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a, Excluded | Nothing: not searchable, not shown | Transcript and passages, restorable | No |
| b, Excerpts only | Passages in answers, no transcript page | Everything | No |
| c, Full (default for talks) | Passages and the full transcript page | Everything | On request |
| d, Owner only | Nothing | Everything, for the teacher's use | Yes |
| e, Export and delete | Nothing | Nothing: the takedown runs after the export | Yes, before deletion |
| f, Full removal | Nothing | Nothing, with permanent exclusions, as in rule 4 above | No |

Levels a to c are what the public sees. Levels d to f are what happens behind it. The full transcript holds the teacher's words and each student question in its lightly edited form, labeled as such. Chant, announcements and sitting appear as marked gaps. Transcripts are reviewed offline in v1: a reviewer such as one of the teacher's priests gets a file per talk with the text and links to each moment of the recording, marks corrections, and the owner imports them as a reviewed version that no later automated pass overwrites.

## Dana mechanic

Dana flows directly from donor to sangha, and the product handles no money and takes no cut. The framing is dependent origination: giver, receiver and gift appear together on one screen as parts of one act.

- Every talk passage links to its sangha's own donation page.
- The dana panel on each answer offers an equal split across the sanghas cited in that answer. The donor enters a total, sees the division, and each line opens that sangha's donation page with the amount suggested. Equal across cited sanghas, not across the whole corpus, keeps the gift connected to the teaching received.
- The site's own dana line is separate and visible, a choice the donor makes, never a share taken from the others. The site has operating costs.
- Recipients are deduplicated at the sangha level, so a sangha cited five times in one answer is one recipient.
- Destination rule: the donation page the source video points to, with a teacher's own page as a second option where one exists.
- A passage whose donation link is unverified, or whose sangha asked not to be listed, renders with attribution but no give button, and drops out of the split.
- An answer draws on at most five to eight distinct sources, so splits stay meaningful and answers stay readable.
- Texts and their publishers sit outside the split.
- A sangha whose talks are used under a mandate can still receive gifts through its own verified page, and its notice says so.
- A true single-gift split through a payment connector is a v3 candidate, for claimed recipients only. It raises tax-deductibility and money-transmission questions that need counsel first.

v1 tests whether any tester follows a dana link. With a small group, read the click count as a floor test, not a conversion rate.

## Ranking and integrity rules

Because money follows citation, every ranking choice moves dana between sanghas, so the rules below are fixed in the spec.

- Relevance to the question is the only ranking signal. No boost for claimed profiles, uploaded volume, donation links on file, affiliation with the founder's own sangha, or rights basis.
- Seattle Soto Zen and Everyday Zen are one seed partner among many in the data model and carry no special weight.
- Claiming a profile unlocks corrections, context and archive upload, and nothing else.
- A soft preference for at least two sanghas per answer may be tested because v1 data is concentrated. If it ever overrides relevance, the answer discloses it.
- Summary constraints: every sentence cites the excerpts that support it and passes the verifier, a claim of agreement or difference cites at least two teachers, every citation shows a verbatim quotation taken by code, and nothing appears that no excerpt supports.
- Texts appear in their own labeled section, never count toward the three teachers, and sit outside the dana split. They are searched separately and never change which talks are returned or in what order.
- Lineage data and reference assets never enter ranking.
- Partner-submitted material gets a review step, to catch archives tuned to common queries.
- A text shown as excerpts needs a recorded rights basis for that edition, which can be the owner's mandate. A text without one is a link-out.

## Success metrics and failure criteria

v1 passes when a practitioner's real question returns passages a teacher would stand behind, and fails fast when it does not. Thresholds below are starting guesses to set before the first test, not findings.

| Test | Pass | Fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval faithfulness and relevance, on 50 practitioner-style questions rated by a teacher reviewer | Most questions return passages the reviewer rates faithful and relevant | Fewer than half do |
| Synthesis integrity, on a sample of 50 answers | No invented claim survives the filter | Any invented claim survives |
| Teacher breadth | Three or more teachers on most multi-voice topics, with the share of one- and two-teacher answers recorded | Not a pass or fail; a measurement that sizes the corpus needed for v2b |
| Return use | Testers come back and run repeat queries | Testers try it once and stop |
| Dana link clicks | Some clicks across the group after a few weeks | Zero clicks from a group where each tester ran about ten queries |
| Text quality | Every OCR text that serves passed its short proofread, with at most 1 error in 12 random units, and a sample of text excerpts is rated faithful | A served text above that error line, or a misquote found |
| Rights handling | Every removal request suspended within one business day, nothing served after a takedown, every excerpt labeled with its true basis, and the ledger complete | A passage served after its takedown, permission wording on a mandated passage, or a request unhandled past one business day |

Accessibility is checked at every release against the latest WCAG 3.0 Working Draft. A release passes when every applicable core requirement is met or has a recorded gap, and fails on any keyboard trap, any body-text contrast failure, or horizontal scrolling at 320 CSS pixels. The first tester cohort includes people who use a screen reader and people who navigate by keyboard only.

The 50-question test set is built deliberately around topics with several voices: compassion, anger, the Heart Sutra, the precepts, repentance, practicing within sangha, and discrimination and suffering. It also includes single-teacher topics, a grief question, a named-teacher query for Norman Fischer to test thin coverage, questions that should surface texts, and a named-author query such as Dogen.

Readiness for a wider launch is a separate question, tested later at roughly 15 to 25 Soto centers and a few thousand segmented talks, with at least three teachers in most answers and a fresh review of every mandated source.

## Risks

Risks are ordered by how much they can sink the product and how cheaply they can be checked, which is the order to test them in.

| Synthesis drifts from what teachers said | Have a knowledgeable reviewer mark 20 answers claim by claim as supported, drifting or invented, and keep every summary sentence tied to a quoted line a reader can check |

| Full transcripts of teachers who have not replied go further than excerpts and may offend | Display levels let a teacher narrow to excerpts, nothing public, or removal in one message. Notices say plainly that full transcripts are shown. If a teacher, Kanshin or counsel says stop, the default returns to excerpts the same day (owner's decision of October 7, 2026) |

| A named-teacher answer reads as that teacher's position | Verbatim passages carry the answer, and no summary is written in this mode |
| A fuller summary reads as teachers' beliefs or blends their voices | Sentences report what is said in a dated talk, agreement and difference stay in separate sections, every marker shows a quoted line, and the verifier checks each sentence against its cited excerpts |
| Fetching media or captions at scale conflicts with platform terms | Official APIs for metadata, third-party fetching off by default, media from SSZ storage or the owner's authorized route, polite fetching, and counsel review |
| Warm relationships make an honest no hard to give | Make declining a one-line reply and ask reviewers for unflattering critique |

One more risk sits outside the table. A person in crisis may use the tool as if it were care. The sensitive-question rules lead with places to find help, leave out the summary line and the giving panel, and keep the text of the most serious cases out of the logs.
